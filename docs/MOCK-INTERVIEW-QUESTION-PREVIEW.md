# Mock Interviews — question preview

Current proposed question bank for your review. These are practice questions, not official university exam questions. The reviewed question bank is now published in Mock Interviews.

Students see the scenario only when timed reading begins, and MMI follow-ups during the timed response. This document shows everything together for your review.

## MMI — 8 stations

Each station: 2 minutes to read, then 8 minutes to respond. A full mock uses all eight stations in a shuffled order.

### Station 1: Confidentiality and patient safety

**Timed reading scenario**

You are a medical student on placement. During a break, your friend Sam tells you they have recently been diagnosed with a sexually transmitted infection. Sam is embarrassed and asks you not to tell anyone. Sam then says they do not plan to tell a recent sexual partner because they are worried about being judged and believe the partner is “probably fine”. Consider how you would respond.

**Questions during the response**

1. What are the main ethical and practical issues in this scenario?
2. How would you respond to Sam in a supportive and non-judgemental way?
3. How would you balance Sam’s confidentiality with concern for the sexual partner’s wellbeing?
4. Tell us about a time you had to manage sensitive information, maintain someone’s trust, or navigate a difficult conversation. What did you learn?

### Station 2: Working through disagreement

**Timed reading scenario**

You are organising a community event with other volunteers. Two team members disagree about how to spend the remaining budget. Each has begun dismissing the other’s ideas, and an important deadline is tomorrow. You have been asked to help the group move forward.

**Questions during the response**

1. How would you approach this disagreement?
2. How would you make sure quieter team members are heard?
3. What would you do if the group still could not agree?
4. Describe a time you changed your approach after listening to someone else.

### Station 3: Giving difficult feedback

**Timed reading scenario**

A fellow student asks you to give feedback on a presentation they will deliver tomorrow. They are proud of their work, but you notice that a central claim is unsupported and could mislead the audience. They tell you they are already very anxious about presenting.

**Questions during the response**

1. How would you start this conversation?
2. How would you balance honesty with supporting your colleague?
3. What would you do if they refused to change the unsupported claim?
4. Tell us about feedback you found difficult to hear and how you responded.

### Station 4: Access and practical support

**Timed reading scenario**

You volunteer at a community support service. A visitor has missed several appointments because public transport is unreliable and they cannot easily take time away from work. A colleague describes them as unmotivated. Consider how you would respond within your role as a volunteer.

**Questions during the response**

1. What information would you want before drawing conclusions?
2. How would you respond to your colleague’s comment?
3. What practical next steps could you explore with the visitor?
4. Describe an experience that changed your understanding of another person’s circumstances.

### Station 5: Making a fair decision

**Timed reading scenario**

A student organisation has funding for one of two projects: extending a well-used mentoring programme or starting a new outreach project for students who currently receive little support. Both proposals are credible, but evidence about the likely impact of the new project is limited.

**Questions during the response**

1. How would you compare these proposals fairly?
2. Whose perspectives would you seek?
3. How would you explain the decision to the unsuccessful group?
4. How would you evaluate the decision after it was implemented?

### Station 6: Taking responsibility

**Timed reading scenario**

While helping organise a volunteer roster, you realise you sent an outdated schedule. Several volunteers may arrive at the wrong time, leaving a service short of help. No one has noticed yet, and you are worried the coordinator will lose confidence in you.

**Questions during the response**

1. What would you do first?
2. How would you explain the error to the coordinator and affected volunteers?
3. What would you change to reduce the chance of recurrence?
4. Tell us about a mistake you learned from.

### Station 7: Responding to a concern

**Timed reading scenario**

At a community information session, an attendee says they feel excluded because the presenters use unfamiliar technical language. Another volunteer tells them that everything was explained in the handout. You are nearby and have an opportunity to respond.

**Questions during the response**

1. How would you respond to the attendee?
2. How would you check whether your explanation was useful?
3. How would you discuss this with the other volunteer?
4. Describe a time you adapted your communication to someone else’s needs.

### Station 8: Managing competing demands

**Timed reading scenario**

You have an important assessment approaching and have also committed to helping run a weekend community activity. A team member withdraws at short notice and asks you to take on their responsibilities. You are already struggling to manage your workload.

**Questions during the response**

1. How would you decide what you can realistically take on?
2. How would you communicate your limits to the team?
3. What support or alternatives could you explore?
4. How do you recognise when your usual coping strategies are no longer enough?

## Panel — 12 selectable questions

Individual question: 30 seconds to read, then 3 minutes to respond. A full panel randomly selects ten distinct questions, with 3 minutes per question and no separate reading break, for 30 minutes total.

### Motivation for medicine

1. What has confirmed that medicine is the right path for you?
2. What have you learnt about the realities of working in medicine?
3. How has your understanding of medical practice shaped the way you are preparing for medicine?

### Service and community

1. Tell us about an experience that changed your perspective.
2. Think of a community, service or work experience. What did you notice about the needs of the people involved?
3. What have you learnt through community, service or work experience that you would carry into medicine?

### Setback and growth

1. Tell us about a setback that mattered to you.
2. Think of a meaningful setback you experienced. How did you respond in the moment?
3. Looking back on a meaningful setback you experienced, what would you now do differently?

### Teamwork and responsibility

1. Tell us about a time you helped a team work through a disagreement.
2. How do you recognise when you need to ask for help?
3. What does taking responsibility mean to you when a team makes a mistake?
