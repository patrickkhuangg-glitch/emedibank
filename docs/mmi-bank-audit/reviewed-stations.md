## 001. Child assent before a procedure

**Category:** Consent · children and assent
**Station ID:** `mmi-bank-2026-09-001-child-assent-before-a-procedure`

A 12-year-old who can explain their worries needs a non-urgent blood test. Their parent agrees, but the child is frightened, repeatedly says no and asks for more time. The busy clinician says parental permission is enough and asks staff to hold the child still. Discuss the choices facing the people involved as a medical-school applicant. You are not expected to make clinical decisions or quote legislation.

### Questions

1. What are the main ethical and practical issues in this scenario?
2. How could the clinician respond to the child's fear before proceeding?
3. How should clinicians balance a parent’s authority with a young person’s developing autonomy?
4. Tell us about a time you respected a boundary or helped someone express a preference. What did you learn?

### Preparation guide

**Key considerations:** Assent, calm explanation, alternatives, and avoidance of unnecessary coercion.

**A practical response:** Pause the non-urgent procedure, explore the child's worries and offer explanation, time and appropriate support rather than treating parental agreement as permission to use force. Seek senior advice about consent if disagreement remains.

**Personal reflection:** Use a specific example, explain your own actions and their effect, and identify what you learned or would change. Everyday experience from study, work, volunteering or relationships is valid. Protect other people’s identities.

**Common weakness:** Assuming the child's age alone settles capacity, or treating restraint as a routine response to refusal.
